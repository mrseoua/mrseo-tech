# MrSeo.tech — EU go-to-market: six locale evidence review
**Research date:** 2026-11-04. **Implementation:** draft PR #39, staging/noindex. **Scope:** FR/IT/ES/NL/RO/CZ.

## Methods and limits
- Country industry sizes: FEVAD, CNMC, Politecnico di Milano, Thuiswinkel, ARMO / European E-Commerce Report, APEK/Heureka. **Not directly comparable** because the mix of product, services and transaction definitions differs. NL industry spending is mature/slightly down, not always fast-growing.
- Local search term candidates below come from live agency/service-page terminology, **NOT validated search volumes**. An attempt to retrieve monthly volume/KD/CPC with connected Ahrefs on Nov 4 returned `Insufficient plan` for **all six countries**. No invented keyword figures or rankings.
- Competitor offerings are examples of the positioning buyers already encounter, not rankings or exhaustive competitor sets.
- The score/prioritization is an editorial hypothesis requiring proof via real lead acquisition and sales capacity. No claim of native-speaking employees, EU local address, Google partner status, or documented results on foreign platforms.

## Priority hypothesis (not a validated ROI projection)

1. **Pilot 1 — CZ / RO:** narrow e-shop SEO and Google Ads conversion-data audits. Smaller regional markets but concrete platform-specific problems and easier narrow positioning. Challenge: Czech also expects Sklik, Seznam Shopping Ads, Heureka; Romanian prospects may expect eMAG/Gomag/MerchantPro knowledge. Never sell hands-on channel/platform management before ability is verified.
2. **Pilot 2 — IT / ES:** catalog SEO and Shopping performance, develop focused landing pages and actual proof. Spanish e-commerce ads competitors already sell low-friction fixed-scope audits. Challenge: sufficient native sales support.
3. **Pilot 3 — FR / NL:** mature, sizable but strong local competition. Lead with a premium, specific technical diagnostic or integrations, not broad “agency in France/Netherlands” claims. Challenge: business buyer trust, linguistic quality and legal disclosures.

This is a **test sequence**, not an objective country ranking. If verified native-language coverage or referrals exist elsewhere, reorder.

## Adaptation applied in draft PR #39
- Market-specific SEO titles for homepage, SEO, Ads, Development. Distinguishes `SEA` in Dutch and `PPC` in Czech, e-commerce audit elsewhere.
- 24 new dedicated diagnostic offers (4 landing pages in each of six locales), 3 concrete checks per offer, additional FAQs addressing realistic platform and integration constraints.
- Contact pages prompt for actual platform/market/problem, prohibit credential sharing; existing contact handler and verified business mailbox routing retained.
- Existing real English portfolio is linked **explicitly as English content** to build trust without inventing local testimonials or adding nonexistent translated portfolio URLs.
- New locale country codes, translated privacy, reciprocal hreflang, self canonical and sitemap are already integrated from the earlier PR #39 work.
- No real cases/results fabricated, no fake country offices, no implicit status as certified partner.
- Preserve `noindex` until separate approval and registration.

## Compliance and launch checklist
- Before enabling EU traffic: identify and publish the real controller/legal entity, rights contact and concrete data retention; review processor roles/international transfers, GDPR and country privacy obligations.
- Review tracking/consent before GA4/Google Ads deployment. [CNIL cookie guidance](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/regles/cookies/FAQ) distinguishes limited measurement vs ad trackers; [Dutch AP](https://autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en/themes/internet-and-smart-devices/cookies) stresses meaningful consent; [Spanish AEPD](https://www.aepd.es/preguntas-frecuentes/17-internet-y-redes-sociales/FAQ-1707-importancia-de-las-cookies-en-la-proteccion-de-datos) requires equally visible accept/reject options.
- Ensure 11-language dropdown remains usable on mobile, form posts to same language and mailbox, legal routes and two noindex layers present; visually inspect all 36 new routes before merge.
- Verify real ability to respond to leads in each language; disclose when communication is actually only in English. Native editorial signoff for headlines, idiom and SEO terms required.
- Set distinct market lead labels, CRM attribution (consent-aware), qualified-lead quality checks, and a repeatable response process once the legal entity exists.
- After legal + editorial + QA approvals, deploy with noindex still on; then explicit separate Google indexing launch. A noindexed site cannot acquire normal Google organic leads from these pages.

## First acquisition test after launch approval
Offer one defined low-friction *paid/qualified* diagnostic, not an unspecific “all SEO and ads” service:
- Problem statement, proof from 2-3 representative URL samples or reporting inconsistencies, preliminary scope and non-guaranteed next step.
- Choose one service per market, validate outreach response rate, qualified enquiries, booked calls, signed projects, and economics before expanding. No invented CPL/CAC/ROAS forecast.
- Prioritize concrete work samples and transparent project workflow over vague ranking promises; if language support/portfolio cannot be verified, don't run paid acquisition in that market yet.
